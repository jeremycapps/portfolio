# 3. Conditions authorizing each operation

## ADVANCE

### Meaning

Release the next planned commitment.

### Authorized when

All decision-critical dimensions are FIT:

$$

and:

$$

$$

and material FOG is absent.

Additionally:

$$

and transferability, where relevant, is within the validated range.

### In plain English

> We have sufficient capacity, sufficient evidence, acceptable exposure, and sufficient expected value to make the next planned commitment.

### ADVANCE should **not** mean

It should mean:

> **The evidence supports this particular next increment of commitment.**

# 4. STAGE

This may be the most important operation in STRATOS.

### Meaning

Commit less than originally planned while preserving the ability to learn.

$$
0 < \Delta K_{authorized} < \Delta K_{requested}
$$

### Authorized when

The goal remains attractive, but one of these is true:

- FIT exists but slack is thin;
- material FOG remains;
- transferability is incompletely validated;
- the commitment has high irreversibility;
- learning must occur before the full commitment can be justified;
- operating capacity is demonstrated only at smaller scale.

Formally:

$$
Value \ge Floor
$$

but:

$$

$$

while some smaller commitment satisfies:

$$

\ge
EvidenceRequired(\Delta K_{smaller})
$$

STRATOS should actually calculate or propose:

$$

$$

Example:

> Do not authorize 100 stores. Authorize the next 20 subject to inventory and distribution gates.

That's a much more useful output than “caution.”

# 5. LEARN

### Meaning

Acquire decision-relevant information before increasing irreversible exposure.

### Authorized when

There is material FOG:

$$
FOG_x = true
$$

and resolving it is both:

1. feasible, and
2. decision-relevant.

Meaning:

$$
ValueOfInformation > CostOfLearning
$$

at least directionally.

### Examples

- pilot
- prototype
- test market
- operational trial
- customer research
- integration test
- supplier validation
- technical test
- additional measurement

### Critical constraint

`LEARN` must have a **boundary**.

It cannot mean:

> Do more analysis.

It should look like:

$$

$$

For example:

> Operate 20 stores for two quarters and determine whether inventory accuracy exceeds X and mature-store gross margin reaches Y.

If those facts won't change the decision, don't authorize learning.

# 6. ADD

### Meaning

Increase the capacity available to the commitment.

### Authorized when

There is a COLLISION because:

$$
Capacity_d < Committed_d + Load_d
$$

but:

1. the goal remains above its value floor;
2. the additional capacity can realistically be obtained;
3. adding capacity has better economics than reducing load;
4. the capacity can arrive **before it is required**.

That fourth condition matters enormously.

Hiring 500 people eventually doesn't solve a launch capacity problem today.

Therefore:

$$

\le
TimeToNeed

must hold.

### ADD can mean

- hire people,
- bring in expertise,
- increase funding,
- extend available time,
- secure suppliers,
- add infrastructure,
- add distribution capability,
- acquire technology.

Same basic commitment model, less of it.

### Meaning

### Authorized when

### Meaning

- existing exposure does not yet justify abandonment;

This composability will be useful.
