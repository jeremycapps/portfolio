Organizations rarely lack metrics. The harder problem is knowing which signals describe the same thing—and noticing when they stop agreeing. StratOS makes that divergence visible before the next commitment is made.

The twelve roles resolved into three dimensions.

Three enterprise questions—economics, commitment, and renewal—cross two operating altitudes and two loci of evidence. StratOps asks what machine the organization is building. BizOps asks where the running machine is succeeding or failing. Internal condition records what the operating system says; external consequence records what appears in the customer, market, or environment.

3 × 2 × 2 = 12 poles. The poles are the result of the model, not its premise.

The two sides do not have to agree.

A company can become faster but less trustworthy, cheaper but less resilient, or more automated but worse at exceptions. If both sides are collapsed into one blended metric, the disagreement disappears.

Where is the system telling two different stories?

Klarna makes the divergence concrete.

In early 2024, Klarna reported extraordinary results for its AI support assistant: two-thirds of support chats handled, resolution time reduced from 11 minutes to 2, and substantial projected savings. The pilot worked. Should Klarna deepen the commitment?

Internal condition

Automation, speed, labor economics, and aggregate satisfaction all looked strong.

External consequence

Complex cases still transferred to people, those cases carried different stakes, and the human capacity receiving them was being reduced. Aggregate satisfaction did not establish quality within those high-stakes segments.

The strongest evidence for expansion existed at a different grain from the risk created by it.

StratOS distinguishes three conditions.

FIT

The evidence and constraints support the commitment at the scale demonstrated.

FOG

A material question remains unanswered.

COLLISION

The proposed commitment conflicts with an observed constraint.

Klarna result: FOG + constraint collision.

Hold the next increment—not the AI program.

Establish complex-segment quality, measure backlog age by segment, protect the capacity required for escalations, define rollback conditions, and then reassess.

Finding the problem is only half of the job.

L1–L5 identifies where the organization should act: strategy, business case, implementation, operations, or audit. Across twelve perspectives, those five levels produce sixty accountable outcomes. Some metrics guide; others gate or stop action.
